# BumbleBeam · Puzzle

[puzzle.bumblebeam.org](https://puzzle.bumblebeam.org): the Bitcoin puzzle (the 2015 transaction
`08389f34…cd15` to 256 addresses, the key of #N in [2^(N-1), 2^N - 1]) live from a Bitcoin node.

| Part | What |
|---|---|
| [`api/server.js`](api/server.js) | Node 20+, no dependencies. Reads the first 160 outputs' addresses and their whole history from a node's mempool/esplora REST API, works out for each puzzle: balance, solved (the first spend that took at least half, net of change), public key exposed (a spend a day or more before the solve), claiming (a spend waiting in the mempool). Serves `/api/puzzles`, `/api/puzzles/{n}` (with history), `/api/events` (the radar), `/api/bench` (GET the table of proven runs; a run is proven by `POST /api/bench/challenge` `{kind, rate}`, which hides 8 to 16 keys among 24 targets in about five seconds of work at that rate, and `POST /api/bench/check` `{id, keys}`, which times it on the server; then `POST /api/bench` `{id, info|threads}`, one a minute per client, writes the server's rate), `/api/health`. Optional Telegram posts for radar events |
| [`api/pool.js`](api/pool.js) | The coordinator of the shared search: pieces of 2^33 keys handed out in a secret Feistel-permuted order, reshuffled every 210 blocks from the block hash (a piece given out before is never given out again), 3–7 hidden check keys per piece that a report must name, 90-minute leases kept alive by the pings up to four times that (a report is taken up to 10 minutes after a lease ran out; a piece whose browser went silent for 5 minutes goes back early), expired/failed/released pieces handed out again first, totals, players by payout address, referrals (written down with the invited searcher's first credited piece), rate limits (32 open pieces and 16 live streams per IP; what they do is kept to decide them later: with each lease how many pieces its IP and its address held at once, how often pings stretched it, whether it went silent, and every refusal in `limit_log`; `node api/limits.js [DATA_DIR] [days]` reports it, with what a limit of N per IP or per address would have refused); after a find the pool pauses, and moves on to the next puzzle if the claim is not confirmed in 24 hours; SQLite (node:sqlite), a lease and its chunk written in one transaction. a heartbeat (a searching browser pings every 5 s with its speed, kept in memory; "searching now" = every open piece pinged in the last 30 s) and the live numbers pushed to every open page (Server-Sent Events). `GET /api/pool`, `/api/pool/live` (event stream), `/api/pool/top` (whole addresses), `/api/pool/history?range=24h|7d|30d` (the pool's speed, from the finished pieces), `/api/pool/recent` (pieces finished or failed, new searchers), `/api/pool/me?addr=` (with the searchers it invited), `POST /api/pool/lease` (a payout address is required), `/report`, `/release`, `/ping`. Env: `POOL_SECRET` (else a file in DATA_DIR), `POOL_PAYOUT_ADDRESS` (the pool's 1.1 BTC of a prize), `POOL_CHUNK_BITS`, `POOL_CHECKS_MIN`/`POOL_CHECKS_MAX`, `POOL_LEASE_MINUTES`, `POOL_MAX_OPEN` (per IP), `POOL_PAUSE_HOURS` |
| [`api/finds.js`](api/finds.js) | A found key on the server's side. `POST /api/pool/found` (the lease, the key, the salt, the commitment, the .ots, Slipstream's answer, the signed transaction or none): checks that the key opens the puzzle's address and lies in the lease's piece, that sha256(key ‖ payout ‖ salt) is the commitment, that the transaction spends only the puzzle's coins with valid signatures and pays nobody but the finder (the share) and the pool (the rest but the fees), as version 2 with no lock time (a bad one or none: the server builds the fair split itself); one find of a lease at a time; closes the piece for good (lease status `found`, off the retry list, no more leases of that puzzle while the prize is unspent); records the find without the key; keeps the key, the salt and the signed transaction in memory only; sends it through MARA Slipstream again (never through the node) and, when MARA mines a block without it (read from the coinbase's tag), signs it again at a higher fee rate with the finder's output unchanged; once the claim is 6 blocks deep and every coin it spends is spent, publishes everything. Each accepted find gets the pool's receipt at once (ECDSA over the puzzle, lease, commitment, payout, finder's satoshis, txids and time; the public key is `RECEIPT_PUB` in `web/engine/claimkit.js` and on /rules), which the finder's page and /proof verify. The vault: a key that came without a safe claim (the finder's browser could not reach Slipstream; `POST /api/pool/solo` from /play without an address or without Slipstream, and the home page's lucky key) is written down until its prize is spent and the owner told by Telegram, never with the key: sealed in its own file, `DATA_DIR/vault/<address>.key` (`api/vault.js`: AES-256-GCM under `POOL_VAULT_KEY`, the address bound in, 0600 in a 0700 directory), with any claim signed with it before the spend; the database says only that it is there; a lucky key's prize nobody claimed in 24 hours is swept to the pool through Slipstream (sent again, and signed again at a higher fee after a MARA block without it). `POST /api/pool/found/status` `{ lease }` (the finder's view; the lease in the body, never in a logged address), `/api/pool/finds` (public: the commitment at once, the key, salt, piece and claim after the spend), `/api/pool/finds/{commitment}.ots`. Env: `POOL_VAULT_KEY` (64 hex, the vault's key: from the deploy's .env, never on the server's disk; without it a key is made in DATA_DIR, which only testing allows), `POOL_RECEIPT_KEY` (hex, the receipts' key; else a file in DATA_DIR; the server does not start unless its public key is `RECEIPT_PUB`, nor with a bad `POOL_PAYOUT_ADDRESS`, except with `POOL_ALLOW_TEST_KEYS=1` or `POOL_TEST_PUZZLE`), `RELAY_SLIPSTREAM_URL`, `FOUND_CHECK_SECONDS` (60), `FOUND_RESEND_SECONDS` (600), `FOUND_CONFIRMATIONS` (6), `LUCKY_HOURS` (24); `POOL_TEST_PUZZLE` (tests only: search a solved puzzle) |
| [`web/`](web) | The site on [`ui/`](../../ui) with the Bitcoin accent. English only. Pages: `/` (tiles, next target with the pool's live line, odds calculator, radar, all 160 puzzles), `/pool` (the pool's dashboard, `pooldash.js`: tiles, the pool's speed over 24 h / 7 d / 30 d, the way in, the live feed, top searchers, the haystack, the hive), `/pool/<address>` (a searcher's page, `searcher.html` + `pool.js`: their numbers and badges; on your own one the search itself and your invite link with QR), `/rules`, `/proof` (every find: the commitment and its .ots at once, the key, salt, piece and claim once the prize is spent, with Verify in the browser), `/play` (search a puzzle's range on the GPU or CPU; #71 as a lottery ticket), `/learn` (key → address step by step; brute force vs BSGS vs kangaroo raced live), `/bench` (GPU/CPU benchmark, results table). `/puzzle/<n>` (one page for each of the 160: address, prize, key range, public key, how it was solved, every transaction; `puzzle.html` + `puzzle.js`). The live numbers are written into `/`, `/pool`, `/bench`, `/proof` and `/puzzle/<n>` on the server before they are sent (`api/pages.js`: nginx sends those pages through `GET /api/page/<name>`, which takes the page's own HTML from puzzle-web's `/_tpl/`, fills each `<!--ssr:id-->…<!--/ssr-->` with `render.js`'s markup, the same the page scripts draw, and sends it on; with the API down or slow nginx sends the page as it is, `/puzzle/<n>` with a 503), so a crawler or an AI agent without JavaScript gets the numbers and nothing moves on a visitor's first paint; `web/render.js` holds the strings and the markup for both. For crawlers: `robots.txt` (the read-only API open, the redirects, the event stream and one searcher's numbers closed), `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` (the API for agents), a canonical address, a link preview (`og.jpg` from `brand/src/og_puzzle.py`) and structured data in every page's head; searchers' pages carry noindex (tag and header) |
| [`web/engine/secp.js`](web/engine/secp.js) | The reference in plain JS (BigInt): secp256k1, SHA-256, RIPEMD-160, base58check, a batched brute-force searcher. Slow, simple, and what the GPU checks itself against |
| [`web/engine/gpu.js`](web/engine/gpu.js) | The WebGPU engine, own WGSL, generated: field arithmetic on 20×13-bit limbs (a column of twenty limb products fits one u32, so a multiplication is 400 plain multiplies and the carries once); each thread checks the 2K+1 keys around a centre point (C ± i·G share the denominator, one inversion per step, the prefix products in a storage buffer); SHA-256 and RIPEMD-160 on the GPU with the padding folded in, a 65536-bit sieve before the target list. The multiplication and the hashes are straight-line code, the inversion a loop (unrolled it compiled for most of a minute on D3D); a cold compile takes 6–9 s on a laptop. The shape (K and the number of threads) is set by the host on the one compiled shader: `tune()` walks up a ladder of shapes while the card gets faster and one step stays under 25 ms, and the pick is kept per card in the browser (`bb-gpu-cfg`). Dispatches are paced to ~60 ms so a laptop GPU that draws the screen stays responsive, and the next one is always queued before the answer to the last is read (two readback buffers): with an idle gap after every dispatch the driver keeps a laptop GPU at about half its clock (~27 W instead of ~70 W, a fifth of the speed). Self-test before every use: planted keys at the centre, both sides and block edges must be found exactly, nothing without a target |
| [`web/engine/tx.js`](web/engine/tx.js) + [`web/engine/claimkit.js`](web/engine/claimkit.js) | The claim's code, shared by the page and the server: address decoding (P2PKH, P2SH, P2WPKH, P2WSH, P2TR), RFC 6979 ECDSA with low S, a legacy sweep of the puzzle address (checked byte for byte against bitcoinjs-lib), reading a sweep back and checking its signatures; the split (6 of 7.1 to the finder, the rest to the pool, the fees from the pool's part), the fee rate chosen without the finder, one transaction for MARA Slipstream, spending only the prize's own coins (at least 0.001 BTC each: the dust anyone sends to a puzzle address is left), a coin's amount proven from the transaction that made it, a fee bump that keeps the finder's output, the checks of a signed sweep, the commitment sha256(key ‖ payout ‖ salt) and the OpenTimestamps file |
| [`web/found.js`](web/found.js) + [`web/claim.js`](web/claim.js) + [`web/prize.js`](web/prize.js) + [`web/findview.js`](web/findview.js) | A find, in the finder's browser, with no button: the commitment to the OpenTimestamps calendars, the split signed and sent through MARA Slipstream, then the key to `POST /api/pool/found` whatever Slipstream said (without Slipstream the server keeps the key and sends the claim itself, and the page asks the finder to write to support); the coins read from mempool.space and blockstream.info (never our own server, which must not learn of the find before Slipstream), every coin seen by one only or given different amounts checked against the transaction that made it (it must hash to the coin's txid); the key hidden until the claim is 6 blocks deep; every step kept in the browser (`bb-win`), so a reload carries on; when neither Slipstream nor the server can be reached, the finder saves the find sealed for the server alone (the request it would have sent, `engine/seal.js`) and mails it to support, who posts its sealed part as it is: `f=find.json; jq .sealed $f | curl -s -X POST "https://puzzle.bumblebeam.org$(jq -r .path $f)" -H 'content-type: application/json' --data-binary @-` (the answer comes back sealed: the find shows up on /proof and in the finder's page); /play and the home page's lucky key (`prize.js`) send a find to the saved payout address by themselves, or to the vault, and draw it with the same card (`findview.js`). |
| [`tests/`](tests) | Node, no framework: `kit.test.js` (tx.js, claimkit.js), `finds.test.js` (the server's side, in-process), `found-e2e.js` (the whole flow in headless Chromium against the real API, every failure case), `layout.js` (nothing moves while the changed pages load), `seo.js` (what a crawler without JavaScript gets: robots.txt, the sitemap's 167 pages with their canonical, own title, preview and structured data and every live number written in, the 160 puzzles in the home page's HTML, noindex on searchers' pages, the trailing-slash redirect, every link in llms.txt, the pages as they are with the API down), `field.test.js` (the GPU engine's field arithmetic run on exact numbers: every routine against BigInt, and no intermediate value reaches 2^32 even with every limb at its limit), `pool.test.js` (the coordinator: leases and limits, pings, grace, invites, the move past a stuck claim, the live stream, query speed), `bench.test.js` (the benchmark's proof: a full search gets in with the server's timing, an early stop, a made-up id, a form post do not), `gpu.js` (the real GPU engine on a software WebGPU: planted keys, 32 targets at once, several chunks, stop and resume on the same and on another engine shape, Stop then Start at once, the ranges where two equal points would be added, another shape on the same compiled engine, tune). `finder.js`: the find's stand-ins found-e2e.js builds on. The outside world is mocked: `mock.js` (node with miners' coinbase tags, Slipstream for the server) and `outside.js` (calendars, Slipstream, the explorers caught in the browser); nothing reaches a real relay |
| [`ui/vendor/qr-code-styling.js`](../../ui/vendor) | qr-code-styling 1.9.2 (MIT, its licence beside it) for the invite QR, loaded only on your own searcher's page |
| [`web/engine/cpu-worker.js`](web/engine/cpu-worker.js) | Web Worker: brute force, baby-step giant-step, Pollard's kangaroo (parallel herds, distinguished points) on the reference |

## API environment

| Variable | |
|---|---|
| `NODE_API` | the node's REST API base, `https://<node>/api` (required; set on the server, never in the repo) |
| `DATA_DIR` | `state.json` (default `/data`) |
| `POLL_SECONDS` | open puzzles are polled this often (60); solved ones every 30 min |
| `PUZZLE_WEB_URL` | where puzzle-web serves the pages' own HTML for `api/pages.js` (`http://puzzle-web:8080`) |
| `POOL_TRANSPORT_KEY` | 64 hex, a P-256 key: a find (`/api/pool/found`, `/found/status`, `/solo`) comes sealed for it by the page (`web/engine/seal.js`: ECDH with a fresh key per request, HKDF, AES-256-GCM, the path bound in; the answer sealed back), so Cloudflare, a proxy or a log never sees a key or a signed claim; a body in the clear is refused. Its public half is `TRANSPORT_PUB` in seal.js; the server does not start with another key outside testing (`PUZZLE_TRANSPORT_KEY` in the server's `.env`) |
| `POOL_VAULT_KEY` | 64 hex: seals the vault's keys (`api/finds.js`, `api/vault.js`); required, from the server's `.env` (`PUZZLE_VAULT_KEY`), with a copy kept off the server: without it the sealed keys cannot be read |
| `TG_BOT_TOKEN`, `TG_CHAT_ID` | radar posts (solved, claiming in mempool, public key exposed, top-up, next target); off while empty. Posts start only after the first full read, so a fresh start sends nothing |

Deploy: images `bumblebeam-puzzle-web` and `bumblebeam-puzzle-api` from CI (`.github/workflows/images.yml`),
services `puzzle-web` / `puzzle-api` in `deploy/docker/compose-bumblebeam.yaml`, `PUZZLE_*` lines in the server's `.env`.

## Plan

1. **Showcase (live):** the status table, the odds calculator, the radar.
2. **Learning (done):** small puzzles solved in the browser, the key → address chain, brute force / BSGS / kangaroo race.
3. **The WebGPU engine (done):** secp256k1, SHA-256, RIPEMD-160, self-test, the public benchmark page.
4. **The coordinator and the game (done):** pieces with check keys, live stats, players, referral links and QR, the claim through private relays, the rules page.
5. **The find (done):** the commitment timestamped, the split sent by the finder's browser before the server hears of it, the server's checks, fee bumps and public proof (`/proof`).
6. Later: a native client, a Telegram bot, API and MCP.
