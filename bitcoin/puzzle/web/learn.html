How the puzzle works

What a Bitcoin key really is, why the puzzle is so hard, how the solved ones were found and what your own odds are, in plain words and with things to try.

A key is just a number

one-way machine

A Bitcoin address works like a locked mailbox, and its key is just a number. Type any number below and watch it turn into an address. The machine only runs one way. Going from a key to an address takes a millisecond. Going back is something nobody knows how to do. So the only way to open the box is to try numbers until one fits.

    iChange a single digit and the whole address changes. There is no getting warmer. The key of puzzle #N has exactly N binary digits. Try 3 (Puzzle #2), 863317 (Puzzle #20) or 970436974005023690481 (Puzzle #70), then press +1. Each one turns into the real address that once held the prize of that puzzle, and the next number into the address of a stranger.
    What do the steps mean?

    A private key is a secret number. The public key is a point on a curve that everyone uses, reached by adding the starting point of the curve to itself that many times. The address is a short fingerprint of that point. Anyone can check the steps forward. Nobody can run them backward. That one-way street is what keeps every bitcoin safe, and the puzzle keys are only weak because their numbers are deliberately small.

    How big is the haystack?

    every digit doubles it

    Each extra binary digit doubles the number of possible keys, and with it the time to try them all. A 4-digit bike lock has 10,000 combinations, a phone number about 10 billion. Puzzle #71 has 1.18 sextillion, about 160 times all the grains of sand on the beaches of Earth. Slide to race a browser tab and a graphics card through any puzzle.

     

    Three ways to search

    raced live on your device

    We hide a random number, and three methods race to find it, right here on your device. Each dial represents the whole haystack: its ring of comb cells shows where a method is looking, and the arc inside shows how much work it has done so far (on a log scale, with ticks at twice the square root and at half the haystack). Checking one by one only needs the address. The two clever methods need the public key, and that changes everything. They need about twice the square root of the haystack: for a trillion keys, about two million hops instead of half a trillion tries.

    How do the clever ones work?

    Meet in the middle: first, write down every small step along the first stretch of a road, like a mark at every metre of the first kilometre. Then walk the road in one-kilometre strides. At each stop, check whether you are standing on a mark. A million-metre road then takes about a thousand strides instead of a million steps. The price is the list of marks. For big puzzles, it would not fit in any computer.

    Kangaroos: a tame kangaroo starts from a known place and hops along, leaving footprints at a few spots (the lit cells on its dial). The white line is where the number was hidden. A wild kangaroo starts from the secret key and hops by the same rules. Once the wild one lands on a footprint, both follow the same path, and the distance between their starts reveals the key. It takes about as many hops as meet in the middle takes steps, but almost no memory.

    The clue from the creator

    why every fifth puzzle fell

    In June 2019, the creator moved a tiny amount from puzzles #65, #70, #75, and every fifth one up to #160. Spending from an address puts its public key on the blockchain for all to see. That was all the kangaroos needed. #65 fell six days later, and by early July, every one up to #100 was gone. #120, #125, #130, and #135 followed over the next years, even though they are far bigger than #71.

    The other puzzles never revealed their public key. For them, trying numbers one by one is the only known way. Each step up doubles the work, so #71 has four times as many keys as #69, which someone found in April 2025.

    Five puzzles still have a public key in the open: #140, #145, #150, #155, and #160. They are huge even for kangaroos. #140 needs about six times as many hops as #135 did. Every solve, with its date and transaction, is on the radar.
