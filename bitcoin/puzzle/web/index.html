The Bitcoin puzzle

Over 900 bitcoin lie on the blockchain, waiting for whoever finds the right numbers. In 2015, in what became known as the Bitcoin puzzle (or the 1000 BTC puzzle), an unknown creator sent bitcoin to 256 addresses (160 of which still count) and deliberately made the keys easy: the key for puzzle #N is simply a number with N binary digits. The small ones fell within weeks. The creator then kept raising the prizes, and today #N holds about N/10 BTC. From puzzle #65 onwards, every fifth puzzle has its public key in the open, making it far easier to crack. The rest can only be guessed, number by number: puzzle #71, the next one, has 2⁷⁰ keys to try. Everything here is read live from a Bitcoin node, and the odds are honest. Play for the thrill, not for a paycheck.

The easiest unsolved puzzle. Its public key has never been revealed, so the only known way in is to try the keys in its range one by one. Alone, that is a lottery ticket: one RTX 4090 would need about 7,500 years to try them all. The BumbleBeam pool searches it together: each browser is given its own piece of the range, no two browsers get the same piece, and whoever finds the key gets 6 of 7.1 BTC.

Radar

A Bitcoin node checks every open puzzle address once a minute, in new blocks and in the mempool. A spend waiting in the mempool means someone is taking a prize at this very moment, and once it confirms, the puzzle is solved. Small payments into an address are knocks: a sign that someone is keeping an eye on it.
